A simple framework for writing better AI prompts

A prompt is the instruction or question you give an AI system. A structured prompt can reduce ambiguity and make the response easier to evaluate.

⌁

The four-part framework

Goal: What outcome do you want? Context: What background should the assistant know? Task: What exactly should it do? Format: How should the answer be presented?

Example: a vague prompt

“Explain cybersecurity.” This gives very little information about the reader, depth or desired result.

Example: a clearer prompt

“I am a beginner learning computer science. Explain the basics of cybersecurity in simple English, use five short headings, give two everyday examples and finish with a short revision checklist.”

Add boundaries

You can specify word count, reading level, tone, tools available, what to avoid and whether sources are required. Do not include passwords, private records or other sensitive information.

Review the answer

Check whether it meets your goal, whether the facts are supported and whether anything important is missing. Follow up with specific corrections rather than simply asking for a “better” answer.

Try it: Write your next prompt using Goal + Context + Task + Format. Compare the result with your usual prompt.

Final thoughts

Good prompts are clear briefs, not magic phrases. Practice, review and iteration matter more than complicated wording.
